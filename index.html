Desk Buddy

Tap to wake me up. I'll ask for the microphone and the camera. The camera picture stays on this device; it only lets me react to you.

This page isn't on HTTPS or localhost, so the browser will block the microphone and camera. Open it over HTTPS or from localhost.

Settings

Claude

The key is stored only in this browser's local storage on this device, and is sent only to api.anthropic.com. Anyone who can use this device or browser profile can read it. Use a separate key with a low spend limit set in the Claude Console.

Listening

Say a wake word anywhere: "Buddy, what's the time?", "What's the time, Buddy?", or just "Buddy" on its own, then ask after the chime (within 8 seconds). Similar-sounding words count too ("body", "buddie", "birdie" for Buddy), most of all at the start or end. A name of two or more syllables that isn't an everyday word works best.

Conversation mode only works with the camera on; with it off, the wake word is still needed. Either way, after each reply there's a follow-up window (below) when no wake word is needed. Without the wake word, Buddy also answers the TV and other people.

On Android, Chrome plays a short sound each time Web Speech recognition starts, and a web page can't turn that off. "Always" keeps it to one sound each time you start speaking. "Tap to talk only" makes no sound until you press 🎙️. The On-device Whisper engine (Speech engines, below) makes no sound at all. Tip: with Web Speech, the phone can miss the first word, so say the wake word by itself first ("Buddy" … chime … "what's the time?"), or put it at the end ("what's the time, Buddy?").

While Buddy is talking (and for a moment after) the microphone is ignored, so it can't hear itself. To interrupt, tap the eye or press Esc.

Voice

Kokoro-82M is an open-source voice model (Apache-2.0). It runs entirely on this device: the model downloads once from Hugging Face and is kept in this browser. It needs a fairly fast phone; if it can't keep up with real time, Buddy switches to the system voice by itself and says so in the diagnostics strip. Rate applies; pitch doesn't.

"Automatic" picks the best voice this device has (Premium, Enhanced or Neural voices first; Australian, then British, then American English). On iPhone, download better voices in Settings › Accessibility › Spoken Content › Voices › English (choose an Enhanced or Premium voice); Siri's own voices aren't available to web pages.

Camera

With the camera on, the face reacts to what it sees: the eyes follow you, it says hello when you come back, gets sleepy in the dark and wakes when the lights come on. This is worked out on this device from a tiny 160×120 picture, and none of it is sent anywhere. The camera pauses when this page is hidden.

When you ask Buddy something while the camera is on, one picture (640 px, about 30–60 KB) goes to Claude with the question, so it can answer things like "what's this?" or "how do I look?". That adds roughly 400 input tokens per question (width × height ÷ 750). Questions about what Buddy can see ("what am I holding?", "look at this") always send a picture.

Curious glances send one small photo (320 px) to Claude every few minutes while someone is in view, and Buddy comments on it. Each glance uses roughly 100 input tokens for the picture (Anthropic's rule of thumb is width × height ÷ 750), plus the personality prompt and a short reply capped at 300 output tokens (thinking counts towards that cap): about the cost of one short question. These are estimates; check usage in the Claude Console.

Speech engines

On-device Whisper downloads the model once from Hugging Face, then keeps it in this browser. Your voice never leaves the device and there's no beep. It needs a reasonably recent phone, and it takes a second or two to transcribe after you stop talking.
